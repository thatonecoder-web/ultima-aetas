# Characters 
**Status:** Initial Draft  
**Last Updated:** November 25, 2026

---

## 1. Overview

Characters are what make ULTIMA AETAS feel like a living world. Every settlement, road, workplace, and community should contain people with their own lives, problems, relationships, and ambitions.

The game draws inspiration from the character design and world simulation found in **Kingdom Come: Deliverance II** and **Red Dead Redemption 2**. These games demonstrate how much immersion can come from ordinary activities, believable NPC behaviour, contextual interactions, and a world that continues to exist beyond the player's immediate attention.

ULTIMA AETAS aims to build upon these ideas within a slow-burn survival RPG.

NPCs should have reasons to wake up in the morning, go to work, interact with other people, maintain relationships, and make decisions. Their lives should continue whether the protagonist is nearby or halfway across the world.

A character shouldn't feel like a quest marker wearing human clothing. They should feel like someone who belongs in the world.

The character system follows three core principles:

- **Individuality:** Every character has an identity, personality, background, beliefs, and personal motivations.
- **Adaptability:** Characters can change through experiences, relationships, hardships, and the consequences of their decisions.
- **Independence:** Characters have their own lives, responsibilities, and objectives that continue without player involvement.

## 2. Character Design Philosophy

ULTIMA AETAS aims to create believable characters through a combination of personality, environmental interaction, social behaviour, and individual decision-making.

Characters should exist as part of the world's simulation rather than simply waiting for the player to interact with them.

A merchant should have a reason to operate their shop. A farmer should have responsibilities concerning their land and livelihood. A settlement leader should have problems that extend beyond the protagonist's personal interests.

These activities should contribute to the world rather than exist exclusively as background animations.

Character behaviour should be influenced by several factors:

- Personality, beliefs, and values.
- Personal history and previous experiences.
- Relationships with other characters.
- Occupation and daily responsibilities.
- Available resources and living conditions.
- Cultural and social background.
- Current environmental and regional circumstances.
- Perceptions of the protagonist and surrounding communities.

Characters should retain recognizable personalities while remaining capable of changing over time.

### 2.1 Design Inspirations

The character and NPC systems take inspiration from several aspects of KCD2 and RDR2.

**Kingdom Come: Deliverance II**

Particular inspiration is drawn from:

- NPC daily routines and schedules.
- Occupations and interactions with the surrounding world.
- Social hierarchy and reputation.
- Contextual dialogue and character reactions.
- The relationship between everyday activities and immersion.
- Settlements that feel inhabited rather than artificially populated.

**Red Dead Redemption 2**

Particular inspiration is drawn from:

- Dynamic NPC interactions.
- Environmental storytelling.
- Character memory and contextual reactions.
- Companion personalities and relationships.
- NPC responses to the protagonist's behaviour.
- The sense that the world continues beyond the player's immediate activities.

These games serve as design references. ULTIMA AETAS will develop its own systems around its survival mechanics, setting, narrative, and technical requirements.

## 3. The Protagonist

The protagonist is the player's primary character and perspective throughout the game.

Their identity, background, experiences, and decisions should influence how other characters and communities perceive them.

The protagonist is part of the world rather than the centre of every event. Other characters should have their own priorities, and the player should not automatically receive special treatment simply because they are the protagonist.

### 3.1 Identity and Customization

Players should be able to establish their protagonist's identity through character creation and customization.

Potential customization options include:

- Physical appearance.
- Clothing and personal equipment.
- Personal history and background.
- Profession or trade.
- Relevant personal characteristics.

The exact customization options and their influence on gameplay remain subject to further development.

### 3.2 Personal Development

The protagonist develops through their experiences, decisions, and interactions with the world.

Development may involve:

- Learning new skills and acquiring knowledge.
- Establishing and maintaining relationships.
- Building a reputation within different communities.
- Changing beliefs, attitudes, and priorities.
- Adapting to increasingly difficult circumstances.
- Developing new habits and behaviours.
- Experiencing the consequences of previous decisions.

Progression should feel connected to the player's actual experiences.

A character who spends considerable time practising a trade should develop their knowledge and abilities through that experience. Someone who repeatedly encounters difficult social situations may approach future interactions differently.

Character development should emerge naturally through gameplay rather than relying entirely on scripted progression.

## 4. Supporting Characters and NPCs

Supporting characters include companions, allies, civilians, community members, antagonists, and other NPCs encountered throughout the world.

Every NPC should have a reason to exist within their surroundings, regardless of their importance to the main storyline.

### 4.1 Independent Lives

NPCs should maintain their own lives outside their interactions with the protagonist.

Their activities may include:

- Working and maintaining their professions or trades.
- Purchasing, selling, and exchanging resources.
- Visiting shops and other locations.
- Maintaining friendships and relationships.
- Participating in community activities.
- Pursuing personal objectives.
- Dealing with disagreements and personal conflicts.
- Travelling between locations.
- Responding to changes within their communities.

Their activities should reflect their occupation, personality, responsibilities, resources, and circumstances.

For example, a blacksmith might open their workshop in the morning, spend several hours working, trade with a travelling merchant, and close for the evening.

A farmer might spend the morning tending to their land, visit a nearby settlement to purchase supplies, and return home before nightfall.

These routines should create the impression of an actual community where people have responsibilities and places to be.

NPCs should also be capable of deviating from their usual routines when circumstances require it.

A shortage of supplies, an unexpected visitor, a community dispute, or a change in local conditions might influence what someone does during the day.

### 4.2 NPC Autonomy

NPCs should be capable of making decisions without requiring direct player involvement.

Their decisions should be influenced by:

- Personal motivations.
- Personality and beliefs.
- Current circumstances.
- Relationships with other characters.
- Available information.
- Resources and responsibilities.
- Local events and environmental conditions.

For example, a companion might decide to leave a settlement because of a personal disagreement. A merchant might stop travelling along a dangerous route. A resident might relocate after losing access to their livelihood.

These decisions should have understandable motivations.

NPC autonomy should contribute to believable world development rather than creating random events without context.

### 4.3 NPC Roles

NPCs can occupy different roles throughout the world.

| Category | Description |
|---|---|
| Companions | Characters who accompany or cooperate with the protagonist. |
| Civilians | Ordinary inhabitants with their own professions, routines, and responsibilities. |
| Community Leaders | Individuals responsible for managing settlements, organizations, or groups. |
| Specialists | Characters with particular knowledge, skills, or professional experience. |
| Antagonists | Individuals whose motivations or actions conflict with the protagonist's interests. |
| Recurring NPCs | Characters who appear across multiple encounters, locations, or storylines. |
| Historical Figures | Individuals whose actions have influenced the world's history. |

These categories are not mutually exclusive. A companion could also be a specialist, while a civilian could eventually become an important recurring character.

## 5. Daily Routines and NPC Behaviour

Daily routines are an important part of making settlements feel inhabited.

NPCs should have activities that reflect their occupations, responsibilities, and personal circumstances.

### 5.1 Daily Schedules

NPC routines may include:

- Waking up and preparing for the day.
- Travelling to work.
- Performing occupational activities.
- Visiting shops and public spaces.
- Socializing with other residents.
- Eating and resting.
- Returning home.
- Sleeping.

Schedules should vary depending on the character.

A shopkeeper, guard, farmer, and travelling merchant should not share identical daily routines.

Their activities should also reflect the environment they inhabit. A settlement experiencing resource shortages or increased danger may have noticeably different routines from one that remains relatively stable.

### 5.2 Dynamic Behaviour

NPC routines should be flexible enough to accommodate unexpected circumstances.

Characters may abandon or modify their usual activities when something important happens.

Examples include:

- A merchant closing their shop because of a regional threat.
- Residents gathering after an important community announcement.
- A worker staying late to finish an urgent task.
- A traveller changing their route because of dangerous conditions.
- A companion visiting someone they have developed a relationship with.

The goal is to create behaviour that feels responsive rather than entirely scripted.

### 5.3 Environmental Interaction

NPCs should interact with the environments they inhabit.

Depending on their activities, they may use workplaces, interact with objects, visit public spaces, prepare food, maintain equipment, or participate in other everyday activities.

Environmental interactions should reinforce the impression that settlements are functional places rather than decorative locations.

## 6. Character Development and Adaptive Personalities

Characters should be capable of changing as they experience the world.

Their personalities, beliefs, attitudes, and behaviour can evolve through personal experiences, relationships, hardships, and significant events.

Personality development should be gradual and contextual.

### 6.1 Personality Foundations

Every character should have an established personality that influences how they behave and interact with others.

Potential personality characteristics include:

- Temperament.
- Sociability.
- Trustworthiness.
- Risk tolerance.
- Patience.
- Empathy.
- Ambition.
- Adaptability.
- Conflict tolerance.

These characteristics should influence decision-making, dialogue, relationships, and reactions to different situations.

Their implementation may involve numerical attributes, behavioural traits, or a combination of both.

### 6.2 Personality Adaptation

Characters should be capable of developing new attitudes and behavioural patterns through their experiences.

Potential influences include:

- Personal successes and failures.
- Losing important relationships.
- Meeting new people and experiencing different cultures.
- Difficult moral decisions.
- Prolonged hardship.
- Changes in personal circumstances.
- Significant experiences involving the protagonist.
- Changes within their communities.

For example, a character who initially trusts strangers might become more cautious after repeated experiences of betrayal.

Another character might become more cooperative after finding a community where they feel accepted.

These changes should influence future behaviour, including conversations, relationships, and personal decisions.

### 6.3 Consistency and Individuality

Characters should not all react to the same situation in the same way.

Two individuals experiencing the same event might develop completely different perspectives depending on their backgrounds, personalities, beliefs, and previous experiences.

Some characters may change considerably, while others might remain firmly attached to their established beliefs despite external pressures.

Personality adaptation should allow characters to grow without making their behaviour feel disconnected from their established identities.

## 7. Relationships and Social Dynamics

Relationships are an important part of character development and everyday world simulation.

NPCs should be capable of forming relationships with one another independently of the protagonist.

These relationships should develop through shared experiences, disagreements, cooperation, and ordinary interactions.

### 7.1 Relationship Development

Relationships may develop through:

- Shared experiences and activities.
- Cooperation and mutual assistance.
- Personal disagreements and arguments.
- Conflicting beliefs and ambitions.
- Trust, betrayal, and reconciliation.
- Trading and exchanging resources.
- Spending time together.
- Shared responsibilities.

Relationships should be capable of improving, deteriorating, or changing completely over time.

A friendship might develop into a rivalry. Two individuals who initially distrust one another might eventually cooperate out of necessity.

### 7.2 Relationship Types

Potential relationship types include:

- Friendship.
- Family.
- Professional relationships.
- Romantic relationships.
- Rivalry.
- Mutual distrust.
- Hostility.
- Cooperation.
- Dependency.

These are examples of possible relationships rather than a rigid classification system.

Relationships should be allowed to develop in more complicated ways than a simple friendship or hostility value.

### 7.3 Community Relationships

Relationships between individuals should influence the communities they belong to.

For example, a disagreement between two influential settlement members might create tension among residents, affect cooperation, or influence decisions about resource distribution.

Strong relationships between individuals could also encourage cooperation between otherwise disconnected communities.

The player should be able to encounter the consequences of relationships that developed without their involvement.

## 8. Character Memory and Reputation

Characters should remember meaningful experiences and use that information to influence their future behaviour.

Memory and reputation are important for creating continuity between interactions.

An NPC should be capable of recognizing someone they have previously encountered and responding according to their past experiences.

### 8.1 Character Memory

Characters may retain memories of:

- Important interactions with the protagonist.
- Promises and agreements.
- Acts of assistance or betrayal.
- Significant community events.
- Personal conflicts.
- Shared experiences with other characters.
- Important events involving their families or communities.

Not every interaction needs to be permanently remembered.

The game should prioritize experiences that have meaningful consequences for the character or their relationships.

### 8.2 Reputation and Perception

Characters should develop their own opinions of the protagonist.

These opinions may be influenced by:

- Personal experiences.
- Actions they have witnessed.
- Information received from other individuals.
- The protagonist's behaviour within their community.
- Existing relationships and affiliations.

For example, a character who has personally received assistance from the protagonist might view them positively.

Another character who has only heard negative stories might approach them with suspicion.

Different characters should be capable of having completely different opinions about the same individual.

Individual relationships and broader community reputation should remain separate, even though they can influence one another.

### 8.3 Information and Rumours

Information should be capable of spreading between characters.

NPCs may discuss events, exchange information, share personal experiences, or pass along rumours.

However, information should not automatically become common knowledge.

Characters should only know about events they have witnessed, experienced, or learned about through believable sources.

Information may also become distorted as it spreads, creating differences between what actually happened and what people believe happened.

This can influence conversations, reputation, relationships, and community behaviour.

## 9. Character Survival and Mortality

ULTIMA AETAS takes place in an unstable world where survival is never guaranteed.

NPCs should face the same general dangers and environmental pressures that shape the rest of the world.

Their survival should depend on their circumstances, resources, decisions, and the events unfolding around them.

### 9.1 Mortality

Characters may encounter threats arising from environmental conditions, infected, hostile individuals, resource shortages, and other dangers present in the world.

Their survival should be influenced by their individual circumstances and ability to respond to those threats.

Important characters should not automatically be protected simply because they have a role in the narrative.

### 9.2 Character Death

Character deaths should have consequences extending beyond the immediate event.

Depending on the individual and their place within the world, their absence may result in:

- Changes in relationships between surviving characters.
- Emotional responses from friends, family, and companions.
- Changes in community dynamics.
- Loss of valuable knowledge or professional expertise.
- Changes to ongoing objectives and activities.
- Long-term consequences for surrounding communities.

For example, the death of a settlement's only doctor could leave residents without access to medical care.

The death of a community leader could create uncertainty over who takes responsibility for the settlement.

The world should respond to the loss of individuals in ways that make sense within their social and personal circumstances.

### 9.3 Continuity

Characters who survive significant events should retain relevant memories and experiences.

Likewise, communities should adapt when individuals disappear, relocate, or die.

Their responsibilities may be transferred to someone else, relationships may change, and previously established routines may be disrupted.

The absence of a character should be capable of changing the world around them.

## 10. Companion Characters

Companions are supporting characters who may accompany the protagonist throughout certain parts of the game.

They should have their own personalities, motivations, opinions, and relationships.

Companions should feel like individuals travelling alongside the protagonist rather than additional equipment or permanently available gameplay abilities.

### 10.1 Independent Motivations

Companions should have personal objectives that exist beyond following the protagonist.

These objectives may influence their decisions, dialogue, and willingness to participate in certain activities.

Their motivations may occasionally conflict with the protagonist's interests.

### 10.2 Companion Relationships

Companions should develop relationships with the protagonist and other characters through shared experiences.

These relationships may influence:

- Dialogue and conversations.
- Willingness to cooperate.
- Personal trust.
- Disagreements and conflicts.
- Future interactions.
- Personal objectives and decisions.

Relationships should develop through gameplay rather than relying entirely on scripted dialogue.

### 10.3 Companion Autonomy

Companions should retain some independence even when travelling with the protagonist.

Depending on their circumstances, they may express opinions, initiate conversations, respond to surrounding events, and make personal decisions.

The exact extent of companion autonomy requires further design.

## 12. Design Goals

The character system should support the following experiences:

1. NPCs should have believable lives that continue independently of the protagonist.
2. Settlements should feel inhabited through everyday activities, occupations, and social interactions.
3. Characters should develop through experiences rather than arbitrary personality changes.
4. Relationships should emerge naturally through interactions and shared circumstances.
5. NPCs should respond to changes within their environment and communities.
6. Characters should remember meaningful experiences and react accordingly.
7. Information should spread through believable interactions rather than being universally available.
8. Character deaths and disappearances should have lasting consequences.
9. Supporting characters should have personal motivations and ambitions.
10. The world should be capable of producing unexpected but believable character interactions and developments.

These goals should guide the development of NPC AI, daily routines, relationship systems, memory systems, companion behaviour, and other related gameplay mechanics.

## 13. Outstanding Design Questions

The following areas require further exploration before the character systems are fully designed and implemented:

- How should NPCs evaluate their personal goals and decide what to do next?
- How should daily routines adapt to unexpected events?
- How should character memories be stored, prioritized, and retrieved?
- How should personality traits influence NPC decision-making?
- How should information and rumours spread between characters?
- How should relationships develop between NPCs without player involvement?
- How should character development interact with factions and settlement systems?
- How should NPCs handle relocation, changing affiliations, and abandoning previous responsibilities?
- How much of an NPC's daily routine should be actively simulated?
- How should companion autonomy interact with player control?
- How should the game balance NPC simulation depth with performance requirements?

These questions will be explored further during the design of the relevant gameplay and technical systems.
