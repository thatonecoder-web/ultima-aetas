# Characters

> **Purpose:** A living-cast reference for recurring characters and the kinds of people who shape the world of *ULTIMA AETAS*. These profiles establish narrative direction without prescribing a single player route. Names and details may evolve as the campaign develops.

## Character principles

Characters are people first, survivors second. Each has a history, a routine, needs they cannot ignore, and beliefs shaped by what they have lived through. They remember more than the player's most recent choice: reliability, cruelty, generosity, broken promises, and the stories others tell all affect how trust develops.

No character should exist only to deliver a quest or explain the setting. Their loyalties can conflict with their compassion, and their plans can continue while the player is elsewhere. Information is always personal and incomplete; two witnesses may describe the same event differently without either deliberately lying.

## Recurring character concepts

These are campaign-facing foundations, not fixed quest outcomes. Their circumstances and relationships should respond to the evolving world state.

### Mara Venn — the keeper of names

A former records clerk who has made a hand-written register of the people who pass through her settlement. Mara records births, deaths, promises, debts, and disappearances because she believes that being remembered is one of the last forms of dignity people can offer one another. Her ledger is useful, but it is also political: deciding whose story is preserved can make her an ally or an enemy.

**Conflict:** The register contains evidence that a respected local leader concealed the circumstances of a past evacuation. Mara must decide whether to publish the truth when doing so could fracture the community she is trying to preserve.

**At the table:** Observant, measured, and difficult to intimidate. She trusts verifiable details more than dramatic claims.

### Tomas Rook — the practical healer

Tomas learned field medicine before the collapse and now works with improvised supplies, careful triage, and a hard-earned understanding of what cannot be saved. He refuses to promise a cure for the infected. He does insist that the living deserve care even when their prospects are poor.

**Conflict:** His scarce medicines are needed by people on opposing sides of a local dispute. Treating one group first may cost him the trust—or the lives—of another.

**At the table:** Patient with the frightened, blunt with the reckless, and privately exhausted by being treated as an answer to every crisis.

### Ilyan Vale — the road listener

A courier who travels between isolated communities, Ilyan carries messages, repairs, and rumors. He marks safe passages in a private notebook but never treats a route as permanently safe; weather, noise, human choices, and infected movement can change a journey overnight.

**Conflict:** Ilyan has begun withholding certain messages after discovering that accurate information has been used to ambush travelers. The delay protects some people and endangers others.

**At the table:** Sociable by habit, cautious by necessity. He asks who will hear a message before agreeing to carry it.

### Sera Kade — the salvage engineer

Sera can coax useful work from damaged tools, pumps, batteries, and machines most people have given up on. She considers repair a communal skill rather than a private gift, but worries that teaching too openly will make her settlement dependent on equipment it cannot maintain.

**Conflict:** A failing piece of infrastructure could protect the settlement if repaired, but the parts needed for it are also keeping a neighboring site alive.

**At the table:** Curious, practical, and impatient with waste. She is more comfortable explaining a mechanism than explaining how she feels.

### Elias Ward — the reluctant mediator

Once a local public servant, Elias is now asked to settle disputes because he remembers how the old institutions worked. He knows those institutions did not protect everyone equally. He tries to make decisions people can live with, while recognizing that a fair process cannot make every outcome painless.

**Conflict:** A case involving stolen food forces Elias to choose between a consistent rule and an exception for a family facing immediate danger.

**At the table:** A careful listener who asks each party to describe what they need, not only what they want punished.

## Relationships and change

- Trust develops through repeated, observable actions; one dramatic gesture rarely erases a history.
- Characters can disagree with the player and still care about them. A relationship is not a reward meter.
- A character's absence, injury, departure, or death should have consequences for people and routines around them.
- Rumors can alter a character's expectations before the player arrives. The rumor may be incomplete or wrong, but the resulting reaction is real.
- NPCs have boundaries. Threats, manipulation, and gifts do not automatically produce cooperation.
- Characters should have lives beyond the player: obligations, friendships, grief, and plans that advance with time.

## Continuity notes

The cast should reflect the project's core themes: memory, consequence, mutual dependence, and the fragile difference between surviving and rebuilding. Avoid making any one character the sole keeper of truth or the only person capable of solving a community's problems.
