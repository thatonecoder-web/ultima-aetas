# Factions and Organizations
---
***Status:** Initial draft
**Last updated:**November 25, 2026
## 1. Overview

ULTIMA AETAS takes place in a world where civilization has suffered a catastrophic collapse, but human society has not disappeared.

Governments still exist. Some retain control over fortified cities and protected territories, while others operate from exile after losing their original territorial bases. Independent settlements, military organizations, religious movements, commercial networks, criminal groups, and ideological movements have also emerged or survived.

The result is a fragmented political and social environment in which authority is distributed among numerous competing organizations.

Factions are an essential part of the world's structure. They influence territorial control, trade, security, politics, social relationships, resource distribution, and the everyday lives of ordinary people.

Every organization has its own identity, objectives, internal structure, leadership, resources, and relationships with the surrounding world.

Factions are designed to exist independently of the protagonist. They can develop relationships, experience internal disagreements, expand or lose influence, change leadership, and undergo significant transformations without requiring the player's involvement.

### Core design objectives

- Establish a diverse political, economic, social, and ideological landscape.
- Allow multiple forms of authority to coexist.
- Preserve surviving national governments and political institutions.
- Create organizations with distinct identities, motivations, and objectives.
- Support internal disagreements and competing political movements.
- Allow factions to evolve through world events and individual decisions.
- Connect faction behaviour to the political beliefs of their members.
- Ensure that organizations have meaningful relationships with surrounding communities.
- Allow smaller organizations to influence regional events without requiring international power.
- Make faction membership, reputation, and relationships meaningful to gameplay.

## 2. The Faction Classification System

Factions are organized into nine primary categories.

These categories describe an organization's primary identity, purpose, and general area of influence. They do not represent exclusive classifications.

An organization may operate across multiple categories. For example, a religious organization might maintain an armed security division, while a commercial organization might operate its own transportation network.

Each faction receives a primary classification and may have additional secondary classifications.

### 2.1 Governmental

Governmental organizations exercise political authority over populations, territories, and institutions.

They include surviving national governments, regional administrations, provisional governments, independent political authorities, and governments-in-exile.

Common organizational forms:

- National governments.
- Regional administrations.
- Provisional governments.
- Governments-in-exile.
- Independent political authorities.
- Civilian administrative institutions.

Governmental organizations may differ considerably in their political structures, legitimacy, territorial control, and administrative capabilities.

Some maintain constitutional systems and representative institutions. Others operate under centralized, authoritarian, military, or provisional administrations.

### 2.2 Extremist and Insurgent

Extremist and insurgent organizations pursue radical political, ideological, religious, or territorial objectives.

Some seek fundamental political transformation, while others aim to establish independent territories, overthrow existing governments, or impose particular ideological systems.

This category includes organizations that employ terrorism or other forms of political violence.

Common organizational forms:

- Revolutionary movements.
- Separatist movements.
- Religious extremist organizations.
- Terrorist organizations.
- Radical political movements.
- Insurgent organizations.

Not every revolutionary or separatist movement is inherently extremist or terrorist. Classification depends on the organization's actual ideology, objectives, and conduct.

### 2.3 Criminal

Criminal organizations operate through illicit economic, political, and social activities.

Some function as loosely connected networks, while others develop extensive internal hierarchies and regional influence.

Common organizational forms:

- Organized crime syndicates.
- Smuggling networks.
- Black-market organizations.
- Protection rackets.
- Independent criminal groups.
- Illicit trading organizations.

Criminal organizations may cooperate with legitimate businesses, corrupt government officials, independent settlements, or other factions.

Their influence can extend into otherwise stable communities.

### 2.4 Settlements and Communities

Settlement organizations represent independent communities and alliances established around shared territory, resources, and collective survival.

They can range from small agricultural villages to large fortified settlements with developed administrative structures.

Common organizational forms:

- Independent settlements.
- Settlement coalitions.
- Agricultural communities.
- Industrial communities.
- Nomadic communities.
- Regional community alliances.

Some settlements prioritize complete independence, while others participate in broader political, economic, or defensive agreements.

Their internal political systems can vary significantly.

### 2.5 Religious and Spiritual

Religious and spiritual organizations are built around shared beliefs, doctrines, traditions, and spiritual interpretations of the world.

They may provide religious services, humanitarian assistance, education, shelter, healthcare, and community support.

Common organizational forms:

- Religious institutions.
- Humanitarian religious orders.
- Cults.
- Spiritual communities.
- Religious revival movements.
- Independent religious congregations.

Religious organizations are not inherently benevolent or hostile. Their actions depend on their beliefs, leadership, internal culture, and individual members.

### 2.6 Corporate and Industrial

Corporate and industrial organizations control or operate commercial production, manufacturing, infrastructure, technology, and essential services.

Some preserve pre-collapse industrial capabilities, while others emerge from surviving businesses, private organizations, and newly established industrial enterprises.

Common organizational forms:

- Industrial corporations.
- Pharmaceutical organizations.
- Energy providers.
- Technology companies.
- Private infrastructure operators.
- Manufacturing organizations.

Corporate influence may come from control over essential resources, industrial facilities, intellectual property, specialized knowledge, and employment.

Some organizations maintain significant political influence despite possessing little or no formal territorial authority.

### 2.7 Military and Security

Military and security organizations specialize in territorial defence, armed protection, military administration, and organized security.

They may operate under national governments, independent political authorities, private organizations, or their own command structures.

Common organizational forms:

- Surviving national militaries.
- Independent military organizations.
- Private security companies.
- Paramilitary organizations.
- Regional defence forces.
- Veteran organizations.

Military organizations vary in their political loyalties, institutional traditions, internal discipline, and relationships with civilian authorities.

Some defend existing governments, while others establish independent territorial administrations.

### 2.8 Trade and Commerce

Trade and commercial organizations facilitate the exchange, transportation, distribution, and management of goods and resources.

Their influence often comes from controlling important commercial connections and access to essential supplies.

Common organizational forms:

- Merchant associations.
- Trading companies.
- Caravan networks.
- Transportation organizations.
- Financial and resource exchanges.
- Commercial cooperatives.

Trade organizations may operate across national boundaries and maintain relationships with otherwise competing political authorities.

Their economic influence can make them essential partners or powerful sources of dependency.

### 2.9 Transit and Traveller Networks

Transit and traveller organizations connect isolated communities, transport people, maintain communication routes, and provide access to otherwise difficult-to-reach territories.

Some operate openly as independent transportation networks, while others maintain hidden routes and clandestine passage systems.

Common organizational forms:

- Underground passage networks.
- Independent caravans.
- Refugee transportation networks.
- Couriers and messengers.
- Travelling communities.
- Independent guides and transportation groups.

These organizations may transport refugees, merchants, travellers, political defectors, medical personnel, and information.

Their activities can be humanitarian, commercial, political, criminal, or a combination of several.

An organization within this category may maintain safehouses and hidden routes between isolated settlements, functioning as a decentralized network similar in principle to historical underground escape and assistance networks.

Not every transit organization operates for humanitarian purposes. Some charge for passage, transport contraband, exchange information, or provide services to criminal and political organizations.

## 3. Major Faction Concepts

The following organizations represent the initial major faction concepts within ULTIMA AETAS.

Their identities, internal structures, and relationships are provisional and may change as the world's history and political environment develop.

They represent different forms of power and influence rather than a single unified political structure.

### 3.1 The Continuity Directorate

**Primary classification:** Governmental  
**Secondary classification:** Political and administrative  
**Geographic influence:** International, concentrated around participating governments and administrative centres.

#### Overview

The Continuity Directorate is an international institutional network concerned with preserving governmental continuity, coordinating reconstruction, and maintaining cooperation between surviving political authorities.

It brings together surviving civil servants, government representatives, diplomats, technical specialists, and administrative institutions.

Its purpose is to preserve the knowledge and institutional foundations necessary for organized civilization.

The Directorate does not automatically possess sovereignty over participating countries or independent governments.

Its influence depends on diplomatic relationships, institutional cooperation, agreements, and the resources available to its participating members.

#### Objectives

- Preserve administrative and legal knowledge.
- Coordinate international reconstruction efforts.
- Maintain diplomatic communication.
- Support the restoration of public institutions.
- Facilitate cooperation between surviving governments.
- Preserve scientific, medical, and technical knowledge.
- Establish agreements concerning territorial security and resource distribution.

#### Internal branches

1. **Civil Administration**
   - Government administration.
   - Civil documentation and public records.
   - Administrative coordination.
   - Institutional preservation.

2. **Diplomatic Council**
   - International negotiations.
   - Diplomatic representation.
   - Agreements between participating governments.
   - Conflict mediation.

3. **Reconstruction Authority**
   - Infrastructure restoration.
   - Utilities and transportation.
   - Industrial recovery.
   - Technical development.

4. **Public Health Commission**
   - Medical infrastructure.
   - Disease monitoring.
   - Healthcare coordination.
   - Cooperation on infected-related research.

5. **Institutional Preservation Office**
   - Legal archives.
   - Historical documentation.
   - Constitutional and administrative records.
   - Preservation of pre-collapse institutional knowledge.

#### Internal political movements

- **Restorationists:** Advocate restoring pre-collapse institutions and constitutional arrangements.
- **Reformists:** Support institutional continuity while pursuing substantial political reform.
- **Centralists:** Advocate greater coordination and centralized administrative authority.
- **Federalists:** Support distributing political authority among regional administrations.
- **Security Hardliners:** Prioritize security and institutional stability.

#### Relationship with infected

The Directorate generally supports containment, population protection, medical research, and organized responses to infected outbreaks.

Its member institutions may disagree over how much authority should be granted to military and medical organizations.

#### Internal tensions

The Directorate faces a fundamental question: how much of the old political order should be preserved?

Some members believe institutional continuity is essential to restoring civilization. Others believe the collapse demonstrated that many pre-collapse systems require fundamental reform.

### 3.2 The Free Settlements Coalition

**Primary classification:** Settlements and Communities  
**Secondary classification:** Political and humanitarian  
**Geographic influence:** Regional and potentially international through independent settlement agreements.

#### Overview

The Free Settlements Coalition is a decentralized alliance of independent communities that cooperate to protect their autonomy and improve their collective survival.

It operates through agreements between participating settlements rather than a single centralized government.

Its members may cooperate on agriculture, security, medical assistance, transportation, and resource distribution.

The Coalition does not necessarily oppose surviving national governments. Its central concern is protecting the independence of its participating communities.

#### Objectives

- Protect settlement autonomy.
- Encourage cooperation between independent communities.
- Facilitate resource distribution.
- Support agricultural and industrial development.
- Provide humanitarian assistance.
- Establish mutual defence agreements.
- Mediate disputes between participating settlements.

#### Internal branches

1. **Settlement Assembly**
   - Political representation.
   - Inter-settlement negotiations.
   - Collective agreements.
   - Administrative coordination.

2. **Mutual Defence Compact**
   - Defensive cooperation.
   - Security agreements.
   - Emergency assistance.
   - Coordination between local defence organizations.

3. **Agricultural Cooperative**
   - Food production.
   - Livestock management.
   - Seed preservation.
   - Agricultural exchange.

4. **Community Relief Network**
   - Medical assistance.
   - Refugee support.
   - Emergency shelter.
   - Humanitarian distribution.

5. **Mediation Council**
   - Inter-settlement disputes.
   - Negotiation.
   - Arbitration.
   - Conflict resolution.

#### Internal political movements

- **Localists:** Advocate extensive independence for individual settlements.
- **Federalists:** Support stronger shared institutions and collective agreements.
- **Mutualists:** Prioritize cooperation and shared resources.
- **Isolationists:** Advocate limiting external relationships and involvement.

#### Relationship with infected

The Coalition has no universal infected policy.

Individual settlements determine their own approaches based on local conditions, available resources, and political preferences.

Some prioritize containment and organized defence. Others avoid infected territories entirely or cooperate with medical researchers.

#### Internal tensions

The Coalition's greatest internal challenge is balancing collective responsibility with local autonomy.

A settlement experiencing severe shortages may refuse to share resources with another community despite previous agreements.

The Coalition must determine whether collective obligations can be enforced without undermining the independence it was established to protect.

### 3.3 The Meridian Exchange

**Primary classification:** Trade and Commerce  
**Secondary classification:** Corporate and Industrial  
**Geographic influence:** International, particularly around major trade routes and commercial centres.

#### Overview

The Meridian Exchange is an international commercial network that facilitates trade, transportation, resource distribution, and industrial cooperation.

Its influence comes from access to goods, commercial infrastructure, and the connections it maintains between otherwise isolated communities.

It does not necessarily govern the territories in which it operates.

Instead, its power comes from its ability to influence access to essential supplies and economic opportunities.

#### Objectives

- Reconnect isolated markets.
- Maintain commercial transportation routes.
- Support manufacturing and industrial recovery.
- Facilitate resource exchange.
- Establish commercial agreements.
- Develop financial and resource-management systems.
- Maintain relationships with surviving governments and independent settlements.

#### Internal branches

1. **Trade Consortium**
   - Merchant associations.
   - Commercial agreements.
   - Market coordination.
   - Trade representation.

2. **Transportation Authority**
   - Road and railway transportation.
   - Maritime logistics.
   - Commercial routes.
   - Freight coordination.

3. **Resource Management Office**
   - Warehousing.
   - Resource allocation.
   - Extraction agreements.
   - Supply distribution.

4. **Industrial Development Group**
   - Manufacturing.
   - Industrial restoration.
   - Machinery production.
   - Workshop coordination.

5. **Financial Directorate**
   - Commercial accounting.
   - Credit and debt arrangements.
   - Currency agreements.
   - Financial administration.

#### Internal political and economic movements

- **Free Traders:** Advocate commercial competition and reduced restrictions.
- **Industrialists:** Prioritize manufacturing and industrial development.
- **Monopolists:** Support consolidating control over essential commercial resources.
- **Cooperative Merchants:** Advocate protecting smaller businesses and independent traders.

#### Relationship with infected

The Meridian Exchange generally considers infected populations a threat to commercial operations and transportation infrastructure.

Its organizations may support containment, route monitoring, and medical research where these activities contribute to commercial continuity.

Internal disagreements may emerge between those who consider research valuable and those who consider it an unnecessary financial risk.

#### Internal tensions

The Exchange faces recurring disputes over economic power and access to essential resources.

Its commercial networks may become indispensable to communities that lack alternative suppliers.

This creates the possibility of economic dependency, monopolistic practices, and disputes over commercial independence.

### 3.4 The Covenant of the New Dawn

**Primary classification:** Religious and Spiritual  
**Secondary classification:** Humanitarian and educational  
**Geographic influence:** International, through religious communities and humanitarian networks.

#### Overview

The Covenant of the New Dawn is a religious and spiritual movement concerned with preserving humanity's moral, cultural, and spiritual foundations.

Its members believe that rebuilding civilization requires more than restoring infrastructure and political institutions.

The Covenant operates religious communities, educational facilities, shelters, and humanitarian initiatives.

Its internal beliefs and practices vary between congregations and leadership groups.

Some members prioritize charitable assistance and community service, while others advocate a more structured religious and political order.

#### Objectives

- Preserve religious and spiritual traditions.
- Provide humanitarian assistance.
- Maintain educational institutions.
- Support displaced populations.
- Preserve historical and cultural knowledge.
- Establish religious communities.
- Promote moral and spiritual reconstruction.

#### Internal branches

1. **Council of Clergy**
   - Religious leadership.
   - Spiritual guidance.
   - Religious administration.
   - Interpretation of doctrine.

2. **Order of Charity**
   - Humanitarian assistance.
   - Shelters and food distribution.
   - Medical support.
   - Refugee assistance.

3. **Keepers of Knowledge**
   - Education.
   - Literacy.
   - Historical preservation.
   - Preservation of written records.

4. **Pilgrim Communities**
   - Travelling religious members.
   - Outreach.
   - Connections between congregations.
   - Community development.

5. **Doctrine Assembly**
   - Theological discussion.
   - Religious interpretation.
   - Internal doctrinal disagreements.
   - Religious governance.

#### Internal political and religious movements

- **Humanitarians:** Advocate helping people regardless of religious affiliation.
- **Traditionalists:** Support preserving established religious teachings and practices.
- **Reformists:** Believe religious institutions should adapt to changing circumstances.
- **Zealots:** Advocate radical religious and moral transformation.

#### Relationship with infected

The Covenant has no universal interpretation of infected individuals.

Some members view infected people primarily as victims deserving compassion and medical assistance.

Others consider infected populations an existential threat that must be contained or eliminated.

Religious interpretations may coexist with medical and scientific explanations.

#### Internal tensions

The Covenant faces disagreements over the relationship between religious authority and humanitarian responsibility.

A congregation may refuse to abandon an infected person's family, while another may consider such a decision an unacceptable risk to its community.

### 3.5 The Iron Vanguard

**Primary classification:** Military and Security  
**Secondary classification:** Governmental and political  
**Geographic influence:** Regional, concentrated around strategically important territories and fortified regions.

#### Overview

The Iron Vanguard is a militarized political and territorial organization that advocates centralized authority, strict security administration, and organized territorial defence.

Its members believe that the collapse exposed the weaknesses of divided political institutions and inadequate security structures.

The Vanguard prioritizes discipline, military organization, and territorial stability.

It may provide protection, infrastructure, and administrative services to communities under its authority, but its political system can involve compulsory obligations, restrictive regulations, and limited civilian influence.

#### Objectives

- Establish defensible territories.
- Maintain organized security.
- Develop permanent administrative institutions.
- Protect essential infrastructure.
- Expand or consolidate territorial influence.
- Establish strict security regulations.
- Maintain military readiness.

#### Internal branches

1. **High Command**
   - Strategic leadership.
   - Organizational administration.
   - Military doctrine.
   - Internal command structure.

2. **Territorial Guard**
   - Regional security.
   - Territorial administration.
   - Protection of infrastructure.
   - Local defensive responsibilities.

3. **Logistics Corps**
   - Transportation.
   - Equipment management.
   - Supply distribution.
   - Resource administration.

4. **Civil Order Bureau**
   - Civil administration.
   - Public regulations.
   - Government services.
   - Internal enforcement.

5. **Veterans' Council**
   - Military traditions.
   - Institutional experience.
   - Recruitment standards.
   - Internal political representation.

#### Internal political movements

- **Expansionists:** Advocate extending territorial influence.
- **Isolationists:** Prefer consolidating existing territories.
- **Institutionalists:** Support developing permanent civilian institutions.
- **Hardliners:** Advocate maintaining military dominance over political administration.

#### Relationship with infected

The Vanguard generally views infected populations as a permanent strategic threat.

Its policies emphasize territorial containment, population protection, and organized responses to outbreaks.

Some members support medical research, while others consider research a distraction from immediate security priorities.

#### Internal tensions

The Vanguard faces disagreements over the long-term relationship between military and civilian authority.

Some members believe military leadership is necessary during the emergency. Others support gradually transferring administrative responsibilities to permanent civilian institutions.

## 4. Transit and Traveller Organizations

Transit and traveller networks deserve particular attention because of their potential influence over the movement of people, information, and resources.

These organizations connect communities that might otherwise remain isolated.

Their structures and motivations can vary significantly.

### 4.1 The Hollow Passage

**Primary classification:** Transit and Traveller Networks  
**Secondary classification:** Humanitarian and clandestine transportation  
**Geographic influence:** Potentially international.

#### Overview

The Hollow Passage is a provisional concept for a decentralized transportation and assistance network.

It connects isolated settlements and provides passage for individuals who cannot safely travel through established routes.

Its potential activities include transporting refugees, delivering messages, assisting displaced families, and maintaining connections between isolated communities.

It may operate through independent guides, travelling groups, safehouses, and local contacts.

The organization does not necessarily possess a centralized leadership structure.

Different regional networks may operate independently while maintaining informal connections.

#### Potential organizational divisions

1. **Routekeepers**
   - Maintain knowledge of established travel routes.
   - Coordinate regional transportation connections.
   - Preserve route information.

2. **Wayfarers**
   - Independent travelling members.
   - Guides and escorts.
   - Connections between settlements.

3. **Sanctuaries**
   - Temporary shelters.
   - Refugee assistance.
   - Safe accommodation.
   - Humanitarian support.

4. **Couriers**
   - Message transportation.
   - Information exchange.
   - Delivery of documents and supplies.

5. **Liaisons**
   - Relationships with settlements.
   - Negotiations with external organizations.
   - Regional coordination.

#### Internal divisions

Possible disagreements include:

- Whether passage should be free or commercially funded.
- Whether politically persecuted individuals should receive priority.
- Whether criminal fugitives should be transported.
- Whether the network should remain politically neutral.
- Whether regional branches should operate independently.

#### Narrative potential

The Hollow Passage could provide a connection between otherwise disconnected regions.

Its members might possess information about political developments, isolated communities, changing territorial conditions, and emerging threats.

Its reputation would depend heavily on the reliability of its members and the conduct of individual regional networks.

The organization remains provisional and should be developed further before being established as a confirmed major faction.

## 5. Surviving Governments and Governments-in-Exile

### 5.1 General situation

The collapse did not eliminate every national government or political institution.

Some governments retained control over heavily secured population centres, administrative territories, and essential infrastructure.

Others lost control of their original territory but maintained political continuity through surviving leadership, institutions, and external relationships.

The distinction between surviving governments and governments-in-exile is important.

**Surviving governments** retain effective territorial control over at least part of their former national territory.

**Governments-in-exile** maintain claims of political continuity and national authority despite losing effective control over their original territorial base.

Their international recognition, administrative capabilities, and actual influence may vary.

### 5.2 Major surviving government centres

The following locations are established examples of surviving national political centres.

#### Washington, D.C. — United States

Washington, D.C. remains the principal surviving American political centre.

The city operates under an extreme lockdown, with entry and departure prohibited.

It represents the remaining centre of American federal political authority and institutional continuity.

According to the current world concept, it is the only recognized safe haven within the United States.

#### Ottawa — Canada

Ottawa remains the principal surviving Canadian political centre.

Federal political and administrative institutions continue operating within its secured territory.

The city is under strict access restrictions and serves as Canada's remaining national administrative centre.

According to the current world concept, it is the only recognized safe haven within Canada.

#### Moscow — Russia

Moscow remains Russia's principal surviving political centre.

The city retains its remaining national administrative and political institutions within a secured territory.

Its authority beyond the protected area requires further definition.

According to the current world concept, it is the only recognized safe haven within Russia.

#### Berlin — Germany

Berlin remains Germany's principal surviving political centre.

Its surviving governmental institutions continue operating within a secured territory.

The political and administrative status of surrounding regions remains to be established.

According to the current world concept, it is the only recognized safe haven within Germany.

#### Mexico City — Mexico

Mexico City remains Mexico's principal surviving political centre.

Its remaining national institutions operate within a protected urban territory.

Its ability to exercise authority beyond the secured city remains to be established.

According to the current world concept, it is the only recognized safe haven within Mexico.

### 5.3 Political independence

These surviving governments are independent political entities.

Their participation in international organizations, reconstruction agreements, or diplomatic networks does not automatically place them under another faction's authority.

They may cooperate, compete, disagree, or refuse diplomatic engagement.

Their continued existence is an important part of the world's geopolitical structure.

### 5.4 Governments-in-exile

Governments-in-exile may maintain:

- Former national leadership.
- Diplomatic representatives.
- Legal and constitutional claims.
- Surviving military personnel.
- Administrative records.
- International recognition.
- Relationships with foreign governments.
- Political organizations operating outside their original territories.

Some may possess substantial influence despite lacking territorial control.

Others may exist primarily as symbolic or diplomatic institutions.

Their legitimacy and ability to represent their former populations can be contested.

## 6. Political Ideology and Internal Diversity

### 6.1 Core principle

Every individual within ULTIMA AETAS has their own political beliefs.

Faction membership does not automatically determine an individual's ideology, political opinions, or personal convictions.

Political diversity exists within organizations, settlements, governments, military institutions, and communities.

A character's political outlook may differ from the official ideology of their organization.

### 6.2 Political dimensions

The Political Belief System uses multiple ideological dimensions rather than relying exclusively on a single left-right spectrum.

Current proposed dimensions include:

| Dimension | General spectrum |
|---|---|
| Economic organization | Collective ownership to free-market economics |
| Political authority | Decentralization to centralization |
| Social values | Progressive to traditionalist |
| National identity | Internationalist to nationalist |
| Civil liberties | Individual liberty to collective security |
| Institutional outlook | Revolutionary to restorationist |
| Religious governance | Secular to religious governance |

These dimensions are independent and should not be treated as a universal measure of morality.

Political labels such as socialist, liberal, conservative, nationalist, anarchist, communist, and fascist can be used where appropriate.

The world may also develop fictional political ideologies shaped by the collapse and its consequences.

### 6.3 Official ideology, leadership, and membership

Every major faction should distinguish between three levels of political identity.

**Official ideology**

The organization's publicly established political principles and objectives.

**Leadership ideology**

The political beliefs and priorities held by its leadership.

**Membership diversity**

The individual beliefs, loyalties, and political affiliations of its members.

These three levels can conflict.

An organization may officially support representative government while its leadership centralizes authority.

A military organization may contain members who support civilian democracy alongside members who advocate permanent military administration.

### 6.4 Internal political movements

Political movements may emerge within factions.

They can compete for leadership, influence organizational policies, and challenge established institutions.

Movements may:

- Gain or lose membership.
- Form temporary political alliances.
- Split into competing groups.
- Merge with other movements.
- Establish independent organizations.
- Become more moderate or more radical.
- Disappear following political or organizational changes.

Not every political movement becomes an independent faction.

### 6.5 Individual political development

Political beliefs are adaptive.

Individuals may change their beliefs through:

- Personal experiences.
- Relationships and social influence.
- Education and information.
- Political developments.
- Economic circumstances.
- Exposure to propaganda or misinformation.
- Personal reflection.
- Significant historical events.

Political change is contextual.

A character's personality, ideological conviction, knowledge, social environment, and circumstances influence whether and how their beliefs change.

Political beliefs should not change arbitrarily or simply because a character encounters someone with a different opinion.

## 7. Faction Relationships

Factions maintain relationships based on shared interests, political disagreements, economic dependencies, territorial disputes, and historical circumstances.

Relationships are not necessarily permanent.

An organization may cooperate with a political rival because of mutual necessity, while two ideological allies may become competitors over resources or leadership.

### 7.1 Proposed major faction relationships

The following relationships represent initial design directions rather than fixed diplomatic outcomes.

| Organizations | Potential relationship |
|---|---|
| Continuity Directorate and Free Settlements Coalition | Cooperation alongside disputes over sovereignty and administrative authority. |
| Continuity Directorate and Meridian Exchange | Infrastructure cooperation and disagreements over commercial independence. |
| Continuity Directorate and Covenant of the New Dawn | Humanitarian and educational cooperation alongside disagreements over religious authority. |
| Continuity Directorate and Iron Vanguard | Competing claims to political legitimacy and territorial authority. |
| Free Settlements Coalition and Meridian Exchange | Commercial cooperation alongside concerns over economic dependency and monopolies. |
| Free Settlements Coalition and Covenant of the New Dawn | Humanitarian cooperation and occasional doctrinal disagreements. |
| Free Settlements Coalition and Iron Vanguard | Disagreements over local autonomy and military administration. |
| Meridian Exchange and Covenant of the New Dawn | Commercial cooperation and disputes over resource allocation. |
| Meridian Exchange and Iron Vanguard | Trade agreements influenced by territorial security and military administration. |
| Covenant of the New Dawn and Iron Vanguard | Potential security cooperation alongside disagreements over authority and ideology. |

### 7.2 Relationship dimensions

Faction relationships should be tracked through several independent factors:

- Diplomatic relations.
- Economic cooperation.
- Military cooperation.
- Ideological compatibility.
- Territorial disputes.
- Historical grievances.
- Leadership relationships.
- Public attitudes.

A formal alliance does not guarantee mutual trust.

Likewise, ideological disagreement does not automatically prevent cooperation.

### 7.3 Internal and regional relationships

Relationships should also exist below the organization-wide level.

For example:

- Two regional branches of the same faction may compete.
- A local settlement may maintain friendly relations with one government while opposing another.
- Individual members of rival factions may maintain personal relationships.
- A faction's leadership may disagree with its regional commanders.

These differences should be represented where they meaningfully affect the world.

## 8. Geographic Influence and Territorial Authority

Factions vary in geographic reach and territorial control.

Not every organization requires territory to exercise influence.

### 8.1 Geographic classifications

**International**

Organizations with influence, membership, or operations across multiple countries or major regions.

**National**

Organizations primarily operating within a particular country.

**Regional**

Organizations concentrated in a specific geographic region.

**Local**

Organizations operating within a settlement, city, or limited territory.

**Distributed**

Organizations operating through independent branches, communities, or networks without a single central territory.

### 8.2 Forms of influence

Organizations may exercise influence through:

- Direct territorial administration.
- Military presence.
- Economic dependency.
- Diplomatic relationships.
- Religious authority.
- Political representation.
- Control of infrastructure.
- Transportation and communication networks.
- Community support.

Territorial claims and effective territorial control should be recorded separately.

A government may claim authority over a former national territory while possessing effective control over only a single protected city.

Similarly, a commercial network may influence several regions without exercising formal sovereignty over any of them.

## 9. Faction Development and World Simulation

Factions exist independently of the protagonist.

Their development should be influenced by world events, leadership decisions, resource availability, relationships, internal political movements, and regional circumstances.

### 9.1 Organizational development

Factions may experience:

- Leadership changes.
- Internal political disputes.
- Organizational restructuring.
- Recruitment and membership changes.
- Expansion or territorial contraction.
- Economic growth or decline.
- Resource shortages.
- Alliances and diplomatic agreements.
- Internal divisions and defections.
- Dissolution or absorption into other organizations.

### 9.2 Organizational autonomy

Factions should pursue their own objectives.

Their activities should not depend entirely on the protagonist's actions.

A faction might establish a trade agreement, lose a settlement, replace its leadership, or experience an internal political dispute while the protagonist is occupied elsewhere.

### 9.3 Internal disagreements

Factions are not unified political entities in which every member thinks or behaves identically.

Branches may compete for resources, leadership influence, or political authority.

Individual members may disagree with their superiors, question organizational policies, or maintain relationships with rival factions.

These disagreements can lead to negotiations, reform movements, internal crises, or organizational splits.

### 9.4 Consequences of world events

Significant events can influence factions differently.

Examples include:

- Major infected outbreaks.
- Loss of important settlements.

- Military defeats.
- Trade route disruptions.

- Diplomatic agreements.

A single event may strengthen one organization while weakening another.

The effects depend on each faction's circumstances, interests, and capabilities.

## 10. Relationship with the Protagonist

The protagonist is not automatically important to every faction.

Their influence should develop through actions, reputation, relationships, and demonstrated capabilities.

Factions may become interested in the protagonist because of:

- Professional skills.
- Reputation.
- Political affiliations.
- Personal relationships.
- Economic contributions.
- Previous interactions.
- Significant decisions.
- Knowledge or information.

### 10.1 Faction reputation

Reputation should be contextual.

The protagonist may be respected by one organization and distrusted by another.

Their actions may also be interpreted differently by individual members of the same faction.

For example, assisting an independent settlement against compulsory taxation might earn support from decentralists while damaging the protagonist's standing with centralist officials.

### 10.2 Membership and affiliation

The protagonist may eventually establish relationships with or become affiliated with different organizations.

Membership should involve meaningful consequences and responsibilities.

Potential forms of involvement include:

- Employment.
- Trade agreements.
- Civilian membership.
- Military service.
- Religious participation.
- Political support.
- Humanitarian work.
- Informal cooperation.

Membership does not require complete ideological agreement.

Individuals may join organizations for protection, employment, family connections, resources, or personal opportunities.

## 12. Faction Design Principles

All factions should follow several overarching principles.

### 12.1 No organizational hive minds

Members should retain individual beliefs, personalities, relationships, and motivations.

Organizational membership does not erase personal identity.

### 12.2 Ideology does not determine morality

A faction's political or religious ideology does not automatically determine the conduct of its members.

Individuals and organizations should be evaluated through their actual decisions and behaviour.

### 12.3 Practical foundations

Every faction requires a reason to exist.

Organizations need some combination of personnel, resources, territory, infrastructure, relationships, institutional continuity, or public support.

Their influence should have a believable foundation.

### 12.4 Conditional alliances

Alliances are based on circumstances and shared interests.

Organizations may cooperate despite ideological disagreements or compete despite sharing similar beliefs.

### 12.5 Organizational evolution

Factions should change over time.

Leadership, resources, internal disagreements, external threats, and political developments can transform their identities and objectives.

### 12.6 Regional variation

An organization's local branches may behave differently from its central leadership.

Regional circumstances and individual leadership should influence local policies and relationships.

### 12.7 Independent world activity

Factions should continue developing without direct player involvement.

Their activities should contribute to the sense that the world exists beyond the protagonist.

### 12.8 Meaningful consequences

Faction interactions should produce consequences that can influence settlements, communities, political relationships, economic conditions, and individual characters.

## 14. Design Philosophy

ULTIMA AETAS should contain a world in which power, authority, and influence are distributed among competing organizations and individuals.

There is no single organization that represents the entirety of civilization, and no faction possesses universal legitimacy.

Surviving governments, independent settlements, commercial networks, religious movements, military organizations, criminal groups, and ideological movements all contribute to the world's political and social structure.

Their members have their own beliefs, ambitions, loyalties, and disagreements.

Some organizations seek to restore the old world. Others want to build something entirely different. Some are concerned primarily with survival, while others pursue wealth, influence, ideology, or territorial control.

Their actions and relationships should evolve through the passage of time, creating a world that continues to develop independently of the protagonist.

**The world does not revolve around its factions, and its factions do not revolve around the protagonist. They exist because people continue to organize, disagree, cooperate, compete, and attempt to shape the world around them.**
