# 1. Game Overview

## 1.1 Game Identity

ULTIMA AETAS is a dark, open-world zombie survival RPG focused on environmental survival, human relationships, social structures, political conflict, and consequential player decisions.

The game explores humanity's struggle for survival and the transformation of human society following the collapse of civilization.

The world continues to function independently of the player. Communities develop, organizations compete for power, individuals pursue personal interests, and political structures evolve over time.

The player must navigate this changing world while managing their own survival, livelihood, relationships, and reputation.

## 1.2 Gameplay Philosophy

ULTIMA AETAS is designed around a slow-burn gameplay experience.

Players should have the freedom to spend considerable time living within the world before becoming involved in major narrative events.

Ordinary activities form an important part of the experience:

- Practising a profession or trade.
- Finding employment and earning money.
- Maintaining equipment and personal belongings.
- Gathering resources and managing supplies.
- Establishing relationships with other characters.
- Developing a reputation within communities.
- Exploring settlements and surrounding environments.
- Avoiding unnecessary confrontations.
- Learning about the world through observation and interaction.

The game should reward patience, preparation, social awareness, and thoughtful decision-making.

## 1.3 Slow-Burn Narrative Progression

The main campaign gradually introduces increasingly significant conflicts.

Early gameplay emphasizes understanding the world, establishing a livelihood, developing relationships, and learning how different organizations operate.

As the campaign progresses, the player becomes involved in larger events with consequences extending beyond their immediate circumstances.

An example of a major narrative dilemma:

A settlement faces an approaching bandit threat. The player must decide whether to sacrifice a close friend to protect the settlement or protect their friend while risking the settlement's destruction or takeover.

Such decisions should carry lasting consequences for characters, communities, relationships, and the wider campaign.

## 1.4 Major World Powers

The world contains several categories of organizations and independent groups.

| Category | Description |
|---|---|
| Government | Surviving civilian administrations and government institutions |
| Military | Military organizations, commands, and independent military formations |
| Criminal organizations | Gangs, organized crime networks, and other criminal groups |
| Trade guilds | Organizations coordinating professions, commerce, and economic activity |
| Corporations | Commercial organizations seeking to preserve or expand their influence |
| Settlements | Independent survivor communities and established population centres |
| Infected | The persistent environmental and biological threat |

Each organization possesses its own interests, resources, leadership, internal culture, and relationships with other groups.

## 1.5 Social Reputation and Livelihood

The player's reputation influences their opportunities and relationships throughout the world.

Characters may judge the player based on observed behaviour, personal experiences, testimony, rumours, and information shared by others.

The player can develop a positive or negative reputation through actions such as:

- Honouring agreements.
- Completing employment or contracts.
- Helping other survivors.
- Stealing or committing fraud.
- Breaking promises.
- Betraying individuals or organizations.
- Engaging in unnecessary violence.
- Demonstrating reliability and professionalism.

Reputation can influence employment opportunities, commercial transactions, social acceptance, access to locations, and relationships.

The consequences of an action depend on its circumstances, who witnessed it, and how information spreads.

## 1.6 Intended Player Experience

The player should gradually develop an understanding of the world and establish their own place within it.

A successful campaign should create a sense of belonging, responsibility, and investment in the people and communities encountered.

Major narrative events should carry emotional weight because the player has spent time building relationships and understanding what is at stake.

## 1.7 Social Position and Information Access

Information access depends on the player's social position, professional occupation, organizational affiliation, relationships, and involvement in local affairs.

Characters do not automatically disclose information simply because the player asks.

Examples of information access:

| Social position | Typical information access |
|---|---|
| Ordinary civilian | Local events, everyday rumours, immediate surroundings |
| Worker or tradesperson | Economic conditions, employment, shortages, workplace disputes |
| Courier or errand runner | Information encountered through deliveries and organizational interactions |
| Organization member | Information relevant to their responsibilities and position |
| Senior member | Internal discussions, organizational disputes, strategic decisions |
| Leadership | High-level organizational information and sensitive decisions |

Information may be acquired through conversations, employment, observation, investigation, contracts, relationships, documents, official communications, and organizational membership.

Information is not always reliable. Rumours may be incomplete, exaggerated, outdated, misleading, or deliberately fabricated.

The willingness of an individual to share information depends on trust, loyalty, personal interests, confidentiality, fear, and their relationship with the player.

Knowledge and access to information form part of character progression.

## 1.8 Faction Governance and Internal Power

Every faction possesses a governing structure appropriate to its identity, culture, and distribution of authority.

### Government Organizations

Government institutions generally operate through bureaucratic hierarchies and established administrative procedures.

- Decisions pass through departments and chains of command.
- Departments may possess competing priorities and interests.
- Officials may pursue independent political agendas.
- Administrative procedures can delay responses.
- Military leadership may occasionally challenge civilian authority or initiate a coup during periods of instability.

### Criminal Organizations

Criminal organizations generally operate through centralized authority.

- A dominant leader exercises significant control.
- Members are expected to follow established chains of command.
- Personal loyalty influences advancement and protection.
- Rivalries may exist between subordinate leaders.
- Leadership instability can create succession disputes.

### Other Organizations

Different organizations may use different governing systems.

Examples include:

- Trade guilds operating through councils and elected representatives.
- Corporations operating through executive leadership and management hierarchies.
- Settlements governed through civilian councils or informal community leadership.
- Military formations operating through strict command structures.

The official organizational structure may differ from the actual distribution of power.

## 1.9 Political Evolution and Regime Changes

Factions can change their governing structures, leadership, policies, alliances, and internal power distribution throughout the campaign.

Possible transformations include:

- Democratic council becoming a military dictatorship.
- Military administration transitioning into civilian government.
- Centralized criminal organization fragmenting into competing groups.
- Merchant council becoming dominated by corporate interests.
- Independent settlement becoming incorporated into a larger faction.
- Coalition government collapsing into authoritarian rule.

### Factors Influencing Political Change

- Leadership deaths, disappearances, removals, and succession.
- Public dissatisfaction and declining institutional trust.
- Military influence and emergency powers.
- Resource shortages and economic instability.
- External threats, territorial disputes, and alliances.
- Internal rivalries, corruption, and ideological disagreements.
- Player decisions and their consequences.

### Consequences for the Player

Political transformations may affect:

- Laws, restrictions, and punishments.
- Trade, taxation, prices, and resource availability.
- Security, checkpoints, and freedom of movement.
- Employment and available missions.
- Relationships with faction members.
- Treatment of civilians and outsiders.
- Access to information and restricted locations.
- The future of settlements and surrounding regions.

Political transformations should have a history. Characters may remember who supported the previous government, who opposed it, and what happened during the transition.

The player can influence political developments, but factions must also be capable of changing independently of the player.

## 1.10 Player Political Influence and Leadership

The player may progress from an ordinary civilian or outsider into positions of authority within factions and settlements.

Potential positions include:

| Organization | Potential positions |
|---|---|
| Settlements | Council member, mayor, governor |
| Government | Civil administrator, regional official |
| Military | Officer, commander, senior leadership |
| Trade guilds | Representative, council member, guildmaster |
| Corporations | Executive, director |
| Criminal organizations | Lieutenant, regional boss, organization leader |

Access to these positions depends on the faction's structure, the player's relationships, reputation, qualifications, and previous decisions.

### Responsibilities of Leadership

Holding authority introduces responsibilities beyond ordinary survival:

- Managing supplies and resource distribution.
- Establishing or changing policies.
- Appointing trusted individuals.
- Negotiating alliances and agreements.
- Resolving internal disputes.
- Managing security and settlement defence.
- Responding to civilian concerns.
- Handling corruption, disloyalty, and competing interests.

### Consequences of Authority

Leadership changes how the world interacts with the player.

- Decisions affect entire communities.
- People may support, oppose, distrust, or attempt to manipulate the player.
- Policies can benefit certain groups while disadvantaging others.
- Poor leadership can destabilize an organization.
- Successful leadership can improve stability and long-term survival.
- Losing authority can fundamentally change the player's position in the world.

Authority creates responsibility and consequences. It should never function as a purely cosmetic title.

## 1.11 Independent Faction and Settlement Foundation

The player may establish an entirely new settlement or organization, creating an independent narrative branch.

Founding a faction is a major campaign decision that requires specific narrative opportunities and conditions.

The player must encounter one of three major narrative opportunities and either meet its requirements or accept the opportunity when presented.

The specific opportunities, characters, circumstances, and requirements will be documented in the world and lore documentation.

### Founding a Settlement

Potential activities include:

- Recruiting survivors and specialists.
- Finding and securing a suitable location.
- Establishing essential infrastructure.
- Organizing food, water, medicine, and other supplies.
- Creating governing structures and community policies.
- Establishing trade relationships and diplomatic agreements.
- Recruiting security personnel and organizing community defence.

### Independent Narrative Branch

Founding a settlement introduces a distinct narrative progression.

Its storyline may explore:

- The difficulty of building a functioning society.
- Conflicts between individual freedom and collective security.
- Leadership, legitimacy, and political authority.
- Resource allocation and economic development.
- Diplomacy, territorial disputes, and external threats.
- The consequences of becoming responsible for other people's lives.

### Narrative Consequences

The player's faction develops its own political identity, internal conflicts, relationships, and history.

The organization may eventually establish alliances, become a regional power, experience internal instability, or collapse.

## 1.12 Campaign-Altering Faction Foundation

Establishing an independent faction fundamentally changes the main campaign and its eventual ending.

### Narrative Consequences

Faction foundation can influence:

- **Political landscape:** Existing powers must respond to the emergence of a new organization.
- **Faction relationships:** Alliances, rivalries, trade agreements, and hostilities may develop.
- **Territorial control:** The balance of power across regions can change.
- **Character relationships:** Companions and important NPCs may support, oppose, or leave the player.
- **Main storyline:** Major events, missions, conflicts, and character outcomes may change.
- **Campaign ending:** The final outcome can be fundamentally different depending on the player's decisions and the faction's development.

### Narrative Independence and Integration

The faction-foundation branch has its own substantial storyline, characters, conflicts, and dilemmas.

However, it remains connected to the wider world. Other factions continue pursuing their interests, and the player's organization becomes another political actor within that world.

Founding a faction is a campaign-defining decision. Its consequences extend throughout the main storyline and directly influence the final outcome.

The player is not guaranteed success. Their settlement or organization may flourish, become authoritarian, fracture politically, lose public support, or collapse.

# 5. World Structure and Exploration

**Status:** Partially established.

### 5.1 Regional World Structure

ULTIMA AETAS will use multiple large regional open-world maps rather than one seamless global map.

Regions are connected through physical journeys and in-world transportation.

The design takes inspiration from games that use distinct, explorable regional maps connected through narrative and physical travel.

### 5.2 Initial Countries

The initial planned playable countries are:

1. Canada
2. United States
3. Mexico
4. Germany
5. Russia

Five additional countries may be introduced in later development.

### 5.3 Starting Locations

Players may begin their campaign in different countries.

Starting locations influence the protagonist's personal circumstances, background, initial opportunities, campaign progression, and environmental challenges.

The same underlying world chronology should support different starting experiences.

### 5.4 Regional Identity

Each country should have its own environmental, cultural, political, and geographical identity.

Regional differences may include:

- Climate and seasonal conditions.
- Population density.
- Infrastructure and transportation.
- Available resources.
- Local political structures.
- Faction presence.
- Economic conditions.
- Environmental hazards.

### 5.5 Regional Travel

Travel between countries should involve physical journeys.

Potential requirements include transportation availability, supplies, vehicle condition, travel time, and regional conditions.

Regional transitions should preserve continuity in the world and campaign.

# 6. Survival Mechanics

**Status:** Partially established.

The survival system will simulate the player's physical condition and ability to function in a hostile environment.

Planned systems include:

- Hunger.
- Hydration.
- Fatigue.
- Sleep.
- Injury.
- Exhaustion.
- Body temperature.
- Wetness.
- Clothing condition.
- Environmental exposure.

Survival mechanics should interact with one another.

For example, environmental exposure may affect fatigue and physical performance, while inadequate rest may impair the player's ability to perform everyday activities.

---

# 7. Environmental Simulation

**Status:** Partially established.

The world will feature a dynamic environmental simulation.

Planned environmental systems include:

- Ambient temperature.
- Core body temperature.
- Wind.
- Rain.
- Snow.
- Fog.
- Thunderstorms.
- Heatwaves.
- Seasonal changes.
- Environmental visibility.
- Microclimates.

### 7.1 Temperature

Ambient temperature and core body temperature are separate systems.

Environmental conditions influence the player's temperature over time.

### 7.2 Clothing and Wetness

Clothing properties may include:

- Insulation.
- Water resistance.
- Breathability.
- Weight.
- Durability.

Wetness is tracked separately from clothing condition.

Rain, snow, immersion, and environmental exposure can affect clothing wetness and the player's ability to maintain a stable body temperature.

### 7.3 Environmental Effects

Weather influences movement, visibility, comfort, and survival.

Buildings, basements, forests, vehicles, and other environments may create localized differences in temperature and exposure.

Environmental conditions may also influence infected behaviour.

# 8. Infected AI and Behaviour

**Status:** Partially established.

Infected characters should operate through perception and behavioural systems.

Planned behaviours include:

- Wandering.
- Detecting environmental sounds.
- Detecting visual movement.
- Investigating disturbances.
- Pursuing perceived targets.
- Losing track of targets.
- Attacking when sufficiently close.

Infected should not possess omniscient awareness of the player's location.

Their behaviour should be influenced by environmental conditions and their own perception.

Different infected types may possess different behavioural characteristics.

# 12. NPC and Character Systems

**Status:** Partially established.

NPCs are intended to function as persistent individuals rather than disposable background characters.

Characters may possess:

- Names and personal identities.
- Personalities.
- Needs and routines.
- Personal histories.
- Memories.
- Relationships.
- Opinions.
- Motivations.
- Employment and organizational affiliations.
- Individual goals.

Characters should be capable of acting independently of the player.

### 12.1 Character Independence

NPCs may continue their lives through routines, employment, relationships, travel, and interactions with other characters.

The world should maintain a sense of activity beyond the player's immediate surroundings.

### 12.2 Playable Side Characters

Certain missions may allow the player to temporarily control characters other than the main protagonist.

These characters may include companions, members of survivor communities, military personnel, and civilians.

Their equipment, attributes, relationships, motivations, and perspectives may differ from those of the protagonist.

Information experienced by a playable side character does not automatically become known to the protagonist. Information must travel through plausible in-world channels.

### 12.3 Character Death

Side characters may permanently die depending on player decisions and mission outcomes.

Character deaths may influence:

- Campaign progression.
- Relationships.
- Dialogue.
- Available missions.
- Settlement conditions.
- Available resources.
- Character motivations.
- Campaign endings.

Characters should not receive guaranteed plot protection.

# 13. Relationships, Honour, and Reputation

**Status:** Partially established.

### 13.1 Relationships

Relationships develop through interactions, shared experiences, decisions, and the passage of time.

Characters may remember how the player treated them and how the player behaved toward people they care about.

### 13.2 Honour

The game will feature an underlying honour system.

The player will not receive a visible numerical honour score.

Honour reflects the player's conduct and decisions throughout the campaign.

### 13.3 Reputation

Honour and reputation are separate systems.

Honour concerns the player's conduct, while reputation concerns what other people believe about the player.

Reputation is influenced by local knowledge, witnesses, rumours, personal accounts, and information shared between communities.

Rumours may spread slowly and may be inaccurate, exaggerated, or fabricated.

### 13.4 Consequences and Collective Memory

Actions may have delayed consequences.

For example, a child's observation of the protagonist's behaviour may influence how that child perceives them later.

The death of a child due to the player's decisions may have particularly serious social consequences.

Possible reactions include:

- Community distrust.
- Refusal of assistance.
- Denial of entry.
- Personal resentment.
- Companion doubts.
- Rumours and accusations.
- Long-term changes in relationships.

Reactions should depend on circumstances, responsibility, available information, and the perspectives of individual characters.

Time may soften certain opinions, but forgiveness is never guaranteed.

# 14. Missions and Narrative Systems

**Status:** Partially established.

### 14.1 Mission Availability

Mission availability may depend on:

- In-game time.
- Player decisions.
- Relationships.
- Discovered information.
- Faction conditions.
- Settlement conditions.
- Character availability.
- Previous mission outcomes.

### 14.2 Dynamic Missions

Missions may change or become unavailable if ignored.

Characters and organizations continue pursuing their own interests while the player is occupied elsewhere.

The player should retain control over their own pace without being forced to wait unnecessarily.

### 14.3 Narrative Consequences

Major decisions may influence future missions, relationships, faction politics, settlement conditions, and campaign endings.

Consequences may be delayed and indirect.

The game should avoid excessive immediate notifications explaining every consequence.

### 14.4 Emotional Storytelling

ULTIMA AETAS aims to create emotionally significant narrative experiences.

Important emotional themes include:

- Relationships, friendship, family, and separation.
- Sacrifice and difficult decisions.
- Humanity, kindness, and ordinary civilian life.
- Regret, responsibility, and irreversible consequences.

Characters should have lives beyond their immediate involvement in missions.

Ordinary conversations, shared activities, celebrations, disagreements, and quiet moments should help establish meaningful relationships.

Music, dialogue, performances, silence, and narrative pacing should contribute to emotional storytelling.

### 14.5 Horror and Atmosphere

Horror should emerge from three major sources:

1. Humanity: betrayal, desperation, social collapse, and human conflict.
2. The infected: vulnerability, uncertainty, and persistent danger.
3. The world: isolation, weather, scarcity, and environmental hostility.

# 15. World Simulation and Time

**Status:** Partially established.

The world should evolve independently of the player's immediate activities.

Potential simulation systems include:

- Settlement development and decline.
- Character routines and personal activities.
- Faction expansion and political change.
- Building deterioration.
- Resource availability.
- Environmental and seasonal changes.
- Mission progression.
- Relationships and social developments.

### 15.1 Time Progression

The campaign may span extended periods of in-game time.

Years may pass during the campaign, allowing communities, characters, and political organizations to develop or decline.

### 15.2 Pausing and Suspension

The initial single-player experience should pause the world when the player leaves the game.

NPCs, infected, environmental changes, needs, and mission timers should stop progressing while the game is closed or suspended.

When the player returns, the world should resume from its saved state without offline progression.

The player should be able to save and suspend their campaign without being forced to remain in the game.

# 20. Technical Architecture and Performance Requirements

**Status:** Partially established.

### 20.1 Initial Platform

The initial development target is a single-player browser game.

The game should be playable in Google Chrome and compatible modern browsers.

### 20.2 Initial Technology Direction

Proposed technology:

- TypeScript.
- HTML5 Canvas.
- CSS.
- Vite.

Final technical decisions remain subject to prototype testing.

### 20.3 Performance

The browser game should run efficiently on ordinary computers.

The project should prioritize:

- Efficient rendering.
- Modular systems.
- Controlled simulation workloads.
- Appropriate asset optimization.
- Scalable world simulation.
- Efficient NPC and infected updates.

### 20.4 Architecture

The game should separate core gameplay logic from rendering and platform-specific functionality.

This will help preserve the possibility of future adaptation to a desktop platform.

### 20.5 Future Desktop Adaptation

Following the initial alpha, the project may evaluate adapting the game into a first-person desktop experience.

This depends on the success of the alpha, technical feasibility, and the direction of future development.

Desktop packaging and distribution are outside the initial development scope.

### 20.6 Multiplayer

Multiplayer is outside the initial development scope.

Any future multiplayer direction will be considered separately.
