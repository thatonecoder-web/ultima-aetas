# ULTIMA AETAS
### The world ended. History continued.

> **DISCLAIMER:** ULTIMA AETAS is under early development. All concepts, features, mechanics, systems, and content are subject to change.

# 1. Project Identity

ULTIMA AETAS is an independently developed, open-world zombie survival RPG focused on humanity's struggle for survival and the transformation of the human condition following the collapse of civilization.

The project combines grounded realism, simulation-heavy gameplay and cinematic storytelling to create an expansive world where environmental conditions, human relationships, communities and individual decisions shape the experience.

The initial development phase will focus on a top-down, browser-based single-player game. Following the initial alpha, and depending on its success and technical feasibility, the project may be adapted into a first-person desktop experience.

The game aims to deliver a persistent, reactive and emotionally demanding experience in which the world continues to evolve independently of the player.

# 2. Core Premise

Civilization has collapsed.

Governments, institutions, economies and established social structures struggle to survive an unprecedented global catastrophe. The infected threaten what remains of humanity, while environmental conditions, resource scarcity, political instability, and human conflict add further challenges.

Amid the collapse, the player assumes the role of a customizable central protagonist whose journey begins in one of several countries.

The protagonist's nationality, background, starting location and personal circumstances influence their initial experience and campaign progression.

As the campaign develops, the protagonist encounters other survivors, forms relationships, navigates competing interests, and becomes involved in events that extend far beyond their immediate surroundings.

The world exists independently of the protagonist. Communities develop, characters make decisions, relationships change and circumstances evolve.

The player's actions become part of this evolving history.

# 3. Creative Vision

ULTIMA AETAS is intended to deliver an exceptionally dark, grounded and emotionally demanding experience.

Its creative direction is built around three equally important sources of tension.

### 3.1 Humanity

The collapse of civilization exposes the complexities of human behaviour.

Desperation, betrayal, loyalty, fear, compassion, greed and moral compromise influence how individuals and communities respond to the catastrophe.

Humanity itself becomes unpredictable. Survivors may cooperate, exploit one another, establish new communities or abandon the principles they once believed in.

### 3.2 The Infected

The infected represent a persistent threat to survival.

Their behaviour, environmental interactions and perception of the player should create uncertainty and tension. Encounters should encourage awareness, preparation and careful decision-making.

The infected should remain a meaningful threat throughout the experience.

### 3.3 The Natural World

The environment introduces challenges independent of human conflict and the infected.

Extreme temperatures, severe weather, geographical isolation, limited resources and deteriorating infrastructure can make ordinary survival difficult.

The world should feel indifferent to the player's circumstances.

### 3.4 Emotional Direction

The narrative aims to create meaningful emotional connections between players and the characters inhabiting the world.

Four central emotional elements guide the storytelling:

- **Relationships:** Friendships, companionship, family, separation and the development of trust.
- **Sacrifice:** Difficult decisions involving personal risk, loyalty and the wellbeing of others.
- **Humanity:** Ordinary families, children, elderly people, civilians, kindness and everyday life amid extraordinary circumstances.
- **Regret:** Decisions with irreversible consequences, missed opportunities and the lasting effects of past actions.

Major emotional moments should emerge naturally from character development and accumulated experiences.

Ordinary interactions, conversations around campfires, shared meals, disagreements, celebrations and quiet moments of companionship should help establish genuine relationships.

The atmosphere may be extremely dark, but moments of warmth and hope remain important to the emotional experience.

# 4. Intended Player Experience

Players should experience a world that feels persistent, reactive and capable of existing independently of their immediate actions.

The experience should encourage:

- Exploration and discovery.
- Preparation and resource management.
- Environmental awareness.
- Meaningful relationships with other characters.
- Consideration of immediate and long-term consequences.
- Adaptation to changing circumstances.
- Independent decision-making.
- Observation of the world and its inhabitants.

Players should have considerable freedom in how they approach survival, exploration and the campaign.

The game should reward preparation, patience and awareness while maintaining uncertainty and meaningful consequences.

# 5. Realism and Simulation Philosophy

ULTIMA AETAS combines three complementary approaches to realism.

### 5.1 Grounded Realism

The world and its systems should follow believable internal logic.

Environmental conditions, equipment, survival requirements, character behaviour and physical limitations should interact consistently.

The game should avoid unnecessary abstractions where a believable simulation can provide a more meaningful experience.

### 5.2 Simulation-Heavy Gameplay

The game aims to feature interconnected systems in which relatively small variables can contribute to significant consequences.

Examples include:

- Ambient temperature and core body temperature.
- Clothing insulation, water resistance and breathability.
- Wetness and clothing condition as separate variables.
- Wind, precipitation and environmental exposure.
- Hunger, hydration, fatigue and exhaustion.
- Injuries and their effects on movement and performance.
- Indoor and outdoor temperature differences.
- Environmental conditions affecting infected behaviour.

These systems should interact rather than operate as isolated gameplay mechanics.

### 5.3 Cinematic Storytelling

The narrative should use cinematic presentation, music, sound design, character performances and carefully constructed scenes to create memorable experiences.

Cinematic storytelling should complement the world's established logic and simulation systems.

The overall goal is to combine believable survival mechanics with emotionally powerful narrative experiences.

# 6. World and Geographical Scope

The initial geographical scope consists of five countries:

1. Canada
2. United States
3. Mexico
4. Germany
5. Russia

Each country is intended to feature its own substantial explorable region, with distinct geography, climate, infrastructure, settlements, communities and circumstances.

### 6.1 Regional Identity

Each country should provide a distinct environmental and narrative experience.

Potential regional characteristics include:

- **Canada:** Vast wilderness, harsh winters, isolated settlements and long distances between communities.
- **United States:** Large urban environments, suburbs, rural settlements and extensive infrastructure.
- **Mexico:** Desert environments, mountains, dense urban areas and water scarcity.
- **Germany:** Dense cities, industrial infrastructure, forests and interconnected settlements.
- **Russia:** Extreme cold, enormous distances, isolated communities and challenging infrastructure.

These characteristics represent initial creative direction and may evolve during development.

### 6.2 Starting Countries

Players can select any of the five initial countries as their starting location.

The selection influences:

- The protagonist's initial circumstances.
- Personal background and nationality.
- Starting environment and available resources.
- Initial relationships and encounters.
- Campaign progression and chronology.
- Environmental and survival challenges.
- The narrative events experienced first.

Russia is intended to provide the most challenging starting experience due to its environmental conditions, geographical scale, infrastructure challenges and geopolitical circumstances.

Different institutions, communities and individuals should maintain their own perspectives and motivations. Geopolitical relationships should not be reduced to universal assumptions about entire populations.

### 6.3 Regional World Structure

The game will use separate, substantial regional open-world maps rather than one continuous global map.

Countries will be connected through physical journeys and dedicated transitions.

Travel between regions should account for relevant circumstances, potentially including:

- Available transportation.
- Travel time.
- Supplies and preparation.
- Vehicle condition.
- Environmental conditions.
- Encounters along the journey.
- Companion relationships and reactions.
- Narrative events.

Travel should contribute to the gameplay and storytelling experience.

Previously unlocked regions should remain explorable. Countries should not function as disposable campaign chapters.

### 6.4 Future Geographical Expansion

The initial release is intended to establish five countries.

Five additional countries are envisioned for future expansion, bringing the planned geographical scope to ten countries.

The additional locations will be selected and developed as the project progresses.

# 7. Characters and Character Customization

### 7.1 Central Protagonist

ULTIMA AETAS features one central protagonist.

The protagonist is customizable, with character creation intended to cover elements such as:

- Appearance.
- Name.
- Nationality.
- Country of origin.
- Starting location.
- Personal background.
- Pre-outbreak occupation and circumstances.

The protagonist's background should influence their initial circumstances and narrative opportunities.

### 7.2 Supporting Characters

The world contains a diverse population of supporting characters with individual personalities, histories, motivations, needs, relationships and opinions.

Characters should possess lives beyond their immediate interactions with the protagonist.

They may develop relationships, pursue personal objectives, make independent decisions and respond to changes in their surroundings.

### 7.3 Playable Supporting Characters

Certain missions and narrative sequences will temporarily allow the player to control supporting characters.

These characters may include companions, survivors, members of communities, military personnel and civilians.

Playable supporting characters should offer different perspectives and experiences.

Their abilities, equipment, relationships, motivations and circumstances may differ considerably from those of the central protagonist.

Information experienced by a supporting character should not automatically become known to the protagonist. Knowledge must travel through believable narrative channels.

### 7.4 Character Mortality

Supporting characters can permanently die depending on player decisions and mission outcomes.

Their deaths may influence:

- Character relationships.
- Available missions.
- Dialogue and interactions.
- Community dynamics.
- Available resources.
- Companion behaviour.
- Future narrative events.
- Campaign outcomes.

Characters should not receive automatic plot protection simply because they are important to the narrative.

Their survival should be determined by the circumstances of the world and the player's actions.

# 8. Narrative Structure and Consequences

ULTIMA AETAS aims to feature a long-form campaign with branching narrative developments and numerous possible outcomes.

### 8.1 Consequential Decisions

Player decisions should influence the world beyond the immediate moment.

Consequences may emerge gradually and affect characters, communities, relationships, missions and future events.

Some consequences may remain hidden until much later in the campaign.

The game should avoid constantly informing players that a decision has been recorded or that a particular consequence has been triggered.

Players should discover the effects of their actions through the world itself.

### 8.2 Honour and Reputation

The game is intended to feature a hidden honour system.

The player's underlying conduct and the reputation they develop among others are separate concepts.

**Honour** represents the player's accumulated conduct and decisions.

**Reputation** represents what other people believe about the player.

Reputation should be local or regional. Different communities may have different opinions about the same individual.

Information can spread through rumours, conversations, radio broadcasts, newspapers and noticeboards.

Rumours may be inaccurate, exaggerated, misunderstood or deliberately fabricated.

Characters should form opinions based on their own experiences, available information and existing relationships.

### 8.3 Persistent Narrative Consequences

The world should retain the consequences of significant events.

For example, actions witnessed by a child may influence how that child perceives the protagonist in future interactions.

Similarly, the loss of an important character may affect companions, settlements and future campaign opportunities.

The passage of time may change people's opinions, but forgiveness and reconciliation should never be guaranteed.

### 8.4 Multiple Endings

The campaign is intended to feature numerous possible endings.

The design direction favours a collection of major narrative endings supported by variations in:

- Character survival.
- Companion relationships.
- Community development.
- Faction circumstances.
- Player decisions.
- Protagonist circumstances.
- Major world events.

The intention is to create substantial narrative variety without requiring every ending to be an entirely separate campaign.

# 9. Persistent and Evolving World

The world should continue to develop independently of the player's immediate actions.

Communities may grow, deteriorate, relocate or collapse.

Characters may pursue their own objectives, develop relationships, encounter difficulties and make decisions.

Buildings and infrastructure may deteriorate over time.

Environmental conditions and seasonal changes should influence the world.

The simulation should support the possibility of significant changes over extended periods of in-game time.

### 9.1 Time and Player Control

The game is intended to support a persistent world simulation while preserving player control over the experience.

In single-player mode, the player should be able to pause the world and step away.

When paused:

- NPC activity stops.
- Infected movement stops.
- Weather progression stops.
- Environmental changes stop.
- Survival needs stop progressing.
- Active world events stop progressing.
- Mission timers stop progressing.

The world should resume from its suspended state when the player returns.

The game should not simulate offline world progression while the browser is closed.

Players should be able to save and suspend their experience without being forced to remain active.

# 10. Survival and Environmental Systems

Survival is one of the central gameplay pillars.

The player must manage their physical condition while responding to environmental challenges and resource limitations.

### 10.1 Temperature

The game aims to feature a detailed temperature system involving ambient temperature and core body temperature.

Environmental conditions, clothing, activity, wetness and exposure should influence the player's physical state.

Cold conditions may contribute to shivering, reduced coordination, impaired movement and diminished perception.

Hot conditions may contribute to sweating, thirst, fatigue and impaired concentration.

These effects should develop progressively.

### 10.2 Clothing and Equipment

Clothing should provide different forms of environmental protection.

Relevant characteristics may include:

- Thermal insulation.
- Water resistance.
- Breathability.
- Weight.
- Durability.
- Condition.
- Wetness.

Clothing condition and wetness should be tracked independently.

### 10.3 Weather and Environment

The world should feature changing environmental conditions, including:

- Rain.
- Snow.
- Fog.
- Wind.
- Thunderstorms.
- Heatwaves.
- Seasonal changes.

Different environments should create different microclimates.

Buildings, basements, forests, rooftops and vehicles may provide different levels of environmental protection.

Weather should also influence infected behaviour where appropriate.

### 10.4 Physical Needs

The player should manage several interconnected survival requirements, including:

- Hunger.
- Hydration.
- Fatigue.
- Exhaustion.
- Injury.
- Temperature regulation.

The intention is to create a survival system where preparation and environmental awareness are essential.

# 11. Infected Behaviour

The infected should operate through believable behavioural systems.

Their awareness and reactions should depend on environmental information and available stimuli.

Potential behavioural states include:

- Wandering.
- Detecting sounds.
- Observing potential targets.
- Investigating disturbances.
- Pursuing perceived targets.
- Losing track of targets.
- Attacking when appropriate.

The infected should not possess unrestricted awareness of the player's location.

Different infected behaviours and capabilities may create different levels of danger.

The objective is to create encounters that reward observation, preparation and intelligent decision-making.

# 12. Inventory, Equipment and Resources

The game is intended to feature an inventory and equipment system supporting survival, exploration and resource management.

Potential item categories include:

- Food and water.
- Medical supplies.
- Clothing.
- Tools.
- Weapons and ammunition.
- Crafting materials.
- Survival equipment.
- Miscellaneous resources.

Items may possess individual characteristics, conditions and limitations.

The inventory system should support stackable items where appropriate and a grid-based organizational structure.

Equipment decisions should involve meaningful considerations such as weight, condition, usefulness and available storage.

# 13. Social Systems and Communities

Communities are intended to function as evolving social environments rather than static collections of NPCs.

Individuals should possess relationships, opinions, needs, histories and personal motivations.

Community behaviour may be influenced by:

- Available resources.
- Leadership and organization.
- Security.
- Population.
- Relationships with neighbouring communities.
- Major events.
- Player actions.
- Environmental conditions.

The player's conduct may influence how communities respond to them.

Consequences may include changes in trust, cooperation, access to services, available opportunities and social relationships.

# 14. Emotional and Cinematic Storytelling

Emotional storytelling is a central priority of ULTIMA AETAS.

The project aims to create memorable narrative sequences through established characters, meaningful relationships and believable circumstances.

The player should have opportunities to experience ordinary moments alongside major narrative events.

Campfire conversations, shared experiences, disagreements, personal stories and quiet moments should contribute to character development.

Major emotional scenes should be supported by:

- Character writing.
- Voice performances.
- Music.
- Sound design.
- Environmental storytelling.
- Pacing.
- Player-established relationships.
- The consequences of previous decisions.

The objective is to create emotional moments that feel earned through the player's experiences throughout the campaign.

# 15. Technical Direction and Development Roadmap

### 15.1 Initial Alpha: Browser-Based Game

The first development milestone is a playable, top-down 2D, single-player game running in a web browser.

The initial alpha should establish the core gameplay foundation and demonstrate that the central systems can work together.

Development priorities include:

- Responsive player movement.
- Basic world exploration.
- Environmental simulation.
- Survival mechanics.
- Infected behaviour.
- Inventory and equipment.
- Save and load functionality.
- Performance and stability.

The initial implementation should prioritize accessibility, performance and reliability.

The game should avoid unnecessary graphical complexity that could make it difficult to run on ordinary computers.

### 15.2 Post-Alpha Evaluation

Following the initial alpha, the project's progress and technical foundation will be evaluated.

This evaluation will consider:

- Gameplay quality.
- Technical stability.
- Performance.
- Architecture and maintainability.
- Development feasibility.
- Overall project direction.

The outcome of this evaluation will help determine whether the project proceeds toward a first-person desktop adaptation.

### 15.3 Potential Desktop Adaptation

If the initial alpha is successful and the project remains technically and practically viable, the game may be adapted into a first-person desktop experience.

This would represent a significant evolution of the original presentation and gameplay.

The adaptation may require substantial changes to:

- Rendering and graphics.
- Player controls and movement.
- Camera systems.
- Animation.
- Interaction systems.
- Environmental presentation.
- User interface.
- Performance requirements.

The underlying world simulation, character systems, survival mechanics, narrative logic and other reusable foundations should be designed with potential future adaptation in mind.

The first-person desktop version is a conditional future objective. It is not part of the initial alpha's required scope.

### 15.4 Technical Direction

The initial browser implementation is intended to use:

- TypeScript.
- HTML5 Canvas.
- CSS.
- Vite.

The project should use a modular architecture that separates core game logic from rendering and platform-specific functionality.

Core game systems should remain independent of browser-specific APIs wherever practical.

This will help preserve reusable systems if the project progresses toward desktop development.

### 15.5 Single-Player Priority

The initial development scope is single-player.

Multiplayer is not part of the initial alpha and may be considered as a separate future development direction.

The initial experience should prioritize a reliable and complete single-player foundation.

# 16. Design Principles

ULTIMA AETAS is guided by the following principles.

**Grounded Realism**

Systems should follow believable internal logic and maintain consistency across the world.

**Simulation Depth**

Environmental, physiological, social and gameplay systems should interact meaningfully.

**Cinematic Storytelling**

Narrative presentation should create memorable experiences while respecting the world's established logic.

**Persistent Consequences**

Significant player actions should influence future circumstances and world development.

**Human Complexity**

Characters should possess individual motivations, beliefs, personalities and imperfections.

**Player Freedom**

Players should have meaningful control over their approach to survival, exploration and campaign progression.

**Emotional Authenticity**

Relationships, sacrifices, losses and moments of hope should develop through believable experiences.

**Performance and Accessibility**

The initial browser game should remain practical to run on ordinary hardware, with technical complexity introduced carefully and deliberately.

**Modularity and Expandability**

Systems should be designed to support future development, expansion and potential platform transitions.

**Development Discipline**

The initial alpha should establish a functional and stable foundation before the project expands into more ambitious technical and creative objectives.

# 17. Long-Term Vision

The long-term ambition of ULTIMA AETAS is to create an expansive, interconnected survival experience with substantial geographical, mechanical and narrative depth.

The initial five-country scope will establish the foundation for a larger world, with five additional countries envisioned for future expansion.

The project aims to develop an interconnected simulation in which environmental conditions, human behaviour, social structures and individual decisions contribute to an evolving world.

The initial top-down browser-based alpha will serve as the foundation for evaluating the project's direction and feasibility.

If development goes well, the project may evolve into a first-person desktop experience, expanding its presentation and gameplay while retaining the core vision and systems established during initial development.

The ultimate objective is to create a survival RPG in which the player's experiences become part of a much larger history.

ULTIMA AETAS seeks to explore a fundamental question:

*When the structures of civilization disappear, what remains of humanity?*
